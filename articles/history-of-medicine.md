## The history of medicine: The ancient means of medicine compared to how we see and treat the field of medicine today

According to the Dictionary of Oxford Languages, Medicine as a noun is defined as the “science or practice of diagnosing, relieving, and preventing diseases and illnesses through the use of scientific logic, surgical tools, and medicinal intakes.” 

The general idea in the field of medicine is to stop or alleviate the suffering of one person or another either physically or mentally. The uses are tangible medical instruments and the users of these instruments are commonly professional individuals that underwent special medical education, they are called health workers, such as; doctors, nurses, apothecaries, and psychiatrists. 

This field specifically uses the different resources in our natural habitats and makes it significant to the field of medicine. Oftentimes these resources are being used to treat and alleviate one's physical or mental suffering (e.g. herbs, drugs, medical tools & equipment, etc).

Just like other fields in society, medicine also has its own history. We can remember and become familiarised with the concept of evolution, and this is what all and everything around us was invented. Through small changes that underwent consistent fluctuations, things resulted into a significant and profound innovation that could save and make life easier for us. 

The history of medicine might be a too broad a subject to discuss, as any other things when it comes to history. That's because for every place, medicine has evolved differently. 

In ancient times, around 3300 BCE during and beyond the stone ages, illnesses and diseases were commonly believed to be a punishment from the gods, as historians might say. Ancient medicine heavily relied on religious practices and beliefs, and physical wounds were the usual flaws that people at the time could relieve with herbs and tools. The medicines at the times, as evidenced by various medical texts that were discovered, revealed that the treatments widely used are prayers, incantations, healing and protective amulets, as well as salves, ointments, and other medicines. These ages had their fields of medicine entwined with superstitions (Ancient Medicine; Rachel Hajar, 2012; & Wikipedia).

The practices and use of prehistoric medicine first began in the early years of humans about c. 3.3 million years ago to the beginning of the invention of the writing system c. 5000 years ago (A History of Medicine, 1945). And until here, the impact and importance of the field of medicine grew larger.

According to the medical information given by the Edwin Swift Papyrus in 1500 BC, the credited first doctor in all history was the man Imhotep of Egypt, having cured over 200 people with different kinds of disease and illnesses (History of Medicine). Imhotep was also considered as the first architect and engineer, and acknowledged as a philosopher according to the recorded history of his times (2655-2600 BC). 

Following this, Hippocrate(450 - 380 BCE), with his alliance to philosophy and medicine,  claimed that “diseases and illnesses are merely the occurrences from natural phenomena, and can be cured” started the beginning of scientific changes in medicine. Transforming it into a superstitious practice to now a modern scientific analysis.

Galen(129-126 AD), then added to this unintentionally, that diagnosis can be concluded through inspection and examination of symptoms given from the human body.  

And then up until this point from 200 AD to 1200-1499, many scientific discoveries emerged from the field of medicine; many medical encyclopaedias and handbooks were made from different doctors at different times, ophthalmology evolved, few organs were distinguished, and many systematic and strict surgeries took place. By 1500-1799, the circulatory system was explained, new treatments and medicines were made, and the invention of the microscope. By 1800-1899, the stethoscope was invented, vaccines were developed, and surgery kept continuously improving. In the 19th century, various kinds of treatments and new discoveries kept emerging, such as the discovery of human blood types, treatments for mental illnesses, the discovery of insulin, first few surgical transplants took place, genetic engineering was introduced, and many medical equipment was advanced. By the 20th century, things only kept advancing; pancreas (an organ in the human body) was artificially made, the liver and kidney were grown from vitro and stem cells, and the complete sequence of the human genome.

In the present times of medicine, looking back into its history in a summarised form may seem to look like only a little change took place in the field. But it took over thousands, and even more years to make a huge difference from then and now. If people from the ancient times knew what we were doing with the approach of “healing” in “medicine”, they would immediately call us hypocrites then stone us to death. But if they had experienced all the changes happening from 3000 BC to our current era, 2024 they would understand why we should not be. 

Since the majority of the early humans were hunter gatherers, they took treating as a means of bandaging wounds, relieving pain, preventing getting hurt, and trying to survive from the wounds and inflictions (particularly the basics). Then came the ancient era which viewed healing in a religious and physical way, demanding that the gods control their lives. As more logic and philosophy took over in 2000-1000 BC, people in that field drew more conclusions that diseases and illnesses can be manipulated. And then came forth the various macro and micro discoveries that formed the science in medicine, allowing many people the access of acquiring these sacred knowledge, having the power to cure life and alleviate suffering with the use of modern technology and psychology.
