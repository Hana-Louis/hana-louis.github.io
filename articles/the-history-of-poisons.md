# How did we discover poison?

If there's one thing humans are good at, it's probably being able to discover new things. Give a cave man some stones and sticks and next week it may probably have crafted a spear used for hunting.

Ah yes, the thing that apothecaries were obsessed with, where chemists were amused with, and the medics found helpful. These professionals were heavily studying poison in the prime of their lifetime.

Poison, at the time of prehistorical human knowledge, was dominated by animals like snakes and venomous insects. It was not until cave dwellers noticed that there was another source, an unusual source: plants.

This practice of weaponizing nature inevitably passed down through generations, evolving from a simple way to hunt food into a sophisticated tool for warfare and political power. The Ancient Greeks, for instance, dipped their arrows in hemlock poison when facing Persian forces.

But perhaps the most extreme dedication to the craft came from Ancient China and Pontus, where the legendary Emperor Shen Nung and Mithridate VI tasted over hundreds of herbs firsthand to map out the difference between poison and antidote.

Fun fact: After people like Shen Nung and Mithridate VI mapped out what killed and what cured, ruling classes used it for their favourite game of life and death. Unlike traditional fights using loyal soldiers, poison became the silent assassin of choice.

The first official law against poisoning was declared by Lucius Cornelius Sulla in 82 B.C.E. But spoiler alert: the laws didn't stop the crimes. People were still dying left and right, forcing humanity to rethink their approach.

Humans always thought that poisons and medicines were completely separate things. And honestly, in their defense, you wouldn't normally trust something that can kill you. But the 16th-century physician and alchemist Paracelsus changed that perspective forever when he said: "The dose makes the poison."

So, how did we discover poison exactly? Was it curiosity, our desperation for survival, or just our nature of being craftsmen? I think it was a combination of all. Furthermore, the unity of the science behind poisons and medicines became the foundation of modern toxicology.

What the cavemen saw was not just a coincidence, it was a pattern. The moment they recognized it and acted upon it, was the moment that the future of poisons started.
