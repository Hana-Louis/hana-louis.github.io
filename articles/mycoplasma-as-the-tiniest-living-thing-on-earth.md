## Mycoplasma as The Tiniest Living Thing on Earth

Organisms can be humongous and tiny. Almost all of us know that the most humongous creature on earth is the blue whale, right? But in classrooms, the tiniest creatures often don't get the same hype. Insects do get some praise, but they're nowhere near the tiniest. If we only talk about living things, the tiniest would be microorganisms.  

Microorganisms are tiny living things observed in genetically distinct groups, namely,  bacteria, archaea, cyanobacteria, fungi, and viruses. But in this article, we will discard viruses because they are non-living.

Microbial diversity studies had estimated 1 trillion microbial species roaming on earth and around us. The confirmation of such an estimation, however, is difficult to do as there are many more that we have not discovered. This difficulty may be due to its abundance or tiny size, and most importantly because they are invisible to the human eye. For instance, there are 10^9 prokaryotes per gram of soil and sediments, that alone exceeds the number of animal diversity in aquatic environments. 

Some microbiomes can make us ill while some contribute to our biological functions. For instance, excellent services provided by microbiota to society includes fermentation, antibiotic synthesis, or biological nitrogen ﬁxation. While harmful contributions, for instance, cause diseases and infections such as malaria and athlete’s foot.

So we pin-pointed the fact that microorganisms are the tiniest group, however, to whom exactly should we put the crown inside this group? Viruses are known to be the tiniest of them all, but these are not living things because they have no cell. This leaves us with the second tiniest of them all: bacteria.

There’s one parasitic and disease-causing bacteria that fits this size of the crown. Mycoplasma. A spherical filamentous cell with no cell wall, about 300-800 nm range of visibility. It’s tiny, but it can cause AIDs disease and pneumonia, has a slow growth replication, and can never be eliminated even after antibiotic treatments. 

In a world full of bulky and visible animals, we tend to forget how abundant the tiny invisible living creatures inside, beside, under, and above us are. And as great as knowing who the biggest living thing is, it’s also equally great to know the tiniest creature there is.
